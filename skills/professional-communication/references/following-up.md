## Following Up
Four elements make a status check-in land as warm rather than pressuring:

1. **Social opener** — one line to ease into the ask and maintain the relationship (e.g., "Hope your week is going well."). Never skip straight to the ask; it would make the message read as transactional.

2. **Context** — restate what was promised and when (what, and roughly when), specific enough that the recipient doesn't need to look anything up. When context is vague, they are forced to dig before they can answer.

3. **Low-pressure check** — ask for status in a way that assumes things are fine and invites a no-fault "still in progress" answer, rather than questioning whether the process or person is working. "Still on track?" invites a comfortable answer; "is everything working as expected?" invites doubt about competence or the tool. This is the element that determines whether the message reads as understanding or as blame — get this one right even if you compress everything else.

4. **Offer to help** — close with a low-key offer of assistance if useful. Reframes you as someone helping move things along, not just someone waiting.
